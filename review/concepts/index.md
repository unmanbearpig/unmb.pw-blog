# Three directions, one evidence base.

All three concepts use the same verified projects and positioning. The difference is how quickly each one communicates seniority, technical depth and personality.

<div class="concept-review-list">
  <section>
    <p>01 / Recommended</p>
    <h2><a href="/review/concepts/editorial/">Editorial Evidence</a></h2>
    <p>Cold neutrals, cobalt, large project imagery and asymmetric editorial composition.</p>
    <dl><div><dt>Pros</dt><dd>Balances technical proof with a human, product-oriented identity. Strongest fit for both hiring managers and referrals.</dd></div><div><dt>Cons</dt><dd>Requires disciplined image selection and slightly more art direction as new projects are added.</dd></div></dl>
  </section>
  <section>
    <p>02</p>
    <h2><a href="/review/concepts/technical/">Technical Monochrome</a></h2>
    <p>Near-black canvas, pale type, cyan signals and a real system-flow diagram.</p>
    <dl><div><dt>Pros</dt><dd>Makes backend and data depth unmistakable. Memorable for technical interviewers.</dd></div><div><dt>Cons</dt><dd>Can over-index on infrastructure and make the product and visual-identity work feel secondary.</dd></div></dl>
  </section>
  <section>
    <p>03</p>
    <h2><a href="/review/concepts/document/">Minimal Document</a></h2>
    <p>A white dossier with rules, project records and restrained blue accents.</p>
    <dl><div><dt>Pros</dt><dd>Fast to scan, calm and highly credible. Closest to the mental model of a senior CV.</dd></div><div><dt>Cons</dt><dd>Less emotionally distinctive and less able to demonstrate Ivan's visual design range.</dd></div></dl>
  </section>
</div>

The root homepage currently uses Editorial Evidence as the provisional recommendation. No concept has been deployed.
